Exploring Tokyo on Foot: Three Walking Routes

Tokyo is a fantastic city to discover at a slower pace, and what better way to do that than by exploring it on foot? Here are three suggested walking routes, each offering a different feel and allowing you to experience various aspects of the city. Remember to wear comfortable shoes, stay hydrated, and adjust your pace to enjoy the journey! Water and restroom facilities are noted where available.

Route 1: Asakusa and Ueno

This route focuses on the Asakusa and Ueno areas. It begins at Senso-ji, a temple, and continues to Nakamise-dori, a square. The walk then leads into Ueno Park, a large park, before concluding at the Tokyo National Museum, a museum. The total distance is 3.126 kilometers, and the walk is estimated to take approximately 39 minutes and 37 seconds. Drinking water is accessible along the route for refreshment.

Route 2: Harajuku and Yoyogi

If you’re looking for a route with a different vibe, consider this one through Harajuku and Yoyogi. The journey starts at Meiji Shrine, a temple, and progresses to Yoyogi Park, another expansive park. You'll pass by Harajuku Station, a square, before ending at Takeshita Street, also a square. This route covers 3.158 kilometers and is expected to take roughly 41 minutes and 41 seconds to complete. A cafe, グリム原宿店, offers restroom and water facilities nearby.

Route 3: Imperial Palace and Gardens

For a more formal and serene experience, try this route centered around the Imperial Palace. The walk begins at Tokyo Station, a historical structure, then moves to Imperial Palace East Gardens, a park. Next, it passes through Kitanomaru Park, also a park, and finishes at Nippon Budokan, a historical structure. The total distance is 2.679 kilometers, with an estimated walking time of 38 minutes and 32 seconds. No restroom or water points are specifically recorded along this route.

Enjoy your walking adventures in Tokyo!

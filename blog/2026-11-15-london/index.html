# Exploring London on Foot

London is a fantastic city to explore, and what better way to see it than by walking? There are many established routes to help you discover different areas, each offering a unique perspective. Here are three walking routes to consider for your next visit, outlining the stops and estimated walking times. Remember to wear comfortable shoes, maintain a pace that suits you, and take breaks when needed! Staying hydrated is also key, so be sure to plan accordingly.

## Westminster and the River

This route focuses on the area of Westminster and its riverside landmarks. It begins at St James’s Park, a park, and progresses to Westminster Abbey, a basilica. The path then continues to Westminster Bridge, a bridge, before concluding at the London Eye, a monument. The entire walk covers a distance of approximately 2.073 kilometers and takes around 27 minutes and 15 seconds to complete. It’s worth noting that there are no recorded restroom or water points directly along this route.

## Covent Garden and the British Museum

For a taste of central London’s culture and green spaces, try this route. It starts at Covent Garden, a square, and leads to the British Museum, a museum. The walk then passes through Russell Square, a park, before finishing at Bloomsbury Square, another park. The total distance is 2.533 kilometers, with an estimated walking time of 31 minutes and 24 seconds. Like the previous route, no restroom or water points are specifically noted along the way.

## Tower and Southwark

This route explores the historic areas of Tower and Southwark. It begins at the Tower of London, a castle, and continues to Tower Bridge, a bridge. The path then leads to Borough Market, a square, and concludes at Southwark Cathedral, a basilica. This walk is slightly shorter, covering 2.08 kilometers and taking approximately 25 minutes and 44 seconds. A cafe, Plants of Roselyn, is listed as having restroom and water facilities nearby.

Enjoy your London walking adventures!
